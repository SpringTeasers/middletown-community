History

A port town with a long memory

Middletown sits on the banks of the Connecticut River and is the largest city in the Lower Connecticut River Valley Planning Region. It's a place where working waterfront history, a walkable Main Street, and a college-town energy meet.

Middletown began as a busy sailing port, and that maritime past still shapes the city's character. As part of Middlesex County, Middletown served as a county seat from the county's creation in 1785 until county government was eliminated in 1960.

Government

How the city is governed

Middletown operates under a mayor-council form of government, and is currently led by Mayor Gene Nocera.

Mayor–council government

Under this form of government, the city is led by an elected mayor together with an elected council. The mayor leads the city's day-to-day administration and works with the council on budgets and city policy.

Middletown's current mayor is Mayor Gene Nocera.

Who does what

  • Mayor — elected head of the city, leads the administration.
  • City council — elected body that sets policy and approves the budget.
  • Departments — police, public works, community services and more, delivering city services day to day. Contact details are on the Town Services page.
